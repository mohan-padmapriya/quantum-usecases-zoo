# What and why
Involves judging how likely a borrower is to default, and estimating how much a whole loan portfolio could lose. In tests, a quantum model from Crédit Agricole and Pasqal only matched a standard classical model rather than beating it, and quantum versions start to fall apart once they grow past about a dozen qubits. The portfolio-loss side uses the same simulation method as risk analysis, and needs fault tolerant quantum computers to be useful. There is also a practical catch, which is lending decisions legally have to be explainable and checkable for fairness, and the output of a quantum model fits that requirement poorly.
