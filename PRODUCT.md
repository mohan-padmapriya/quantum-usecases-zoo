# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

- **Deep-tech investors and allocators** deciding where to put money or attention. They need a fast, defensible verdict per use case, and the site's credibility has to be strong enough to convince them the author knows the field — academic rigor reads as trustworthiness here.
- **Researchers and technical practitioners** checking specific claims, resource estimates, or classical baselines in their own field.
- **General technically-curious public** browsing to understand what's real vs. hyped in quantum computing.

Primary job-to-be-done across all three: land on the site and quickly find the verdict on one specific use case they care about.

## Product Purpose

A living, critically-sourced, anti-hype reference cataloguing quantum technology use cases across computing, sensing, and communications. Core thesis: no replicated practical quantum advantage exists for any evaluated use case as of mid-2026. Success looks like a reader getting a fast, trustworthy verdict on a specific use case — and the site being credible enough that investors and researchers treat it as a citable source, not a blog.

## Brand Personality

Calm, authoritative, plain-spoken — rigorous and skeptical in substance, never rhetorical or breathless. But explicitly **not** sterile or boring-academic: it should feel like it was made by a sharp, slightly eccentric human scientist with real craft and personality — some delight, some slight motion/animation, a point of view — rather than a corporate template or a dry journal PDF. Rigor with a pulse, not rigor with no pulse.

## Anti-references

- Glossy quantum-computing startup marketing sites: gradient/particle backgrounds, qubit-sphere animations, breathless "quantum advantage" claims, vendor press-release tone.
- Generic SaaS/product landing page templates: oversized hero sections, stock illustration, gradient blobs, marketing-fluff copy.
- Equally avoid the opposite failure mode: a flat, personality-free academic template with zero craft or warmth.

## Design Principles

- **Verdict-first navigation.** Every surface should get a reader to "what's the verdict on X" as fast as possible — filtering and findability beat decoration.
- **Sourced credibility over polish.** Confidence levels, citations, and classical baselines are load-bearing content, never decorative afterthoughts.
- **Rigor with a pulse.** Academically grounded doesn't mean lifeless — craft and personality build trust the way a confident expert's voice does, not a generic template's.
- **Anti-hype by design.** Visual restraint is itself part of the thesis; avoid any pattern associated with quantum-marketing hype, on either the flashy-startup or generic-SaaS end.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA (color contrast, keyboard navigation, semantic structure). No further specific accommodations requested.
